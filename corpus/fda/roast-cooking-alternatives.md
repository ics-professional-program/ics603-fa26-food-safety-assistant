# FDA Food Code §3-401.11(B) — Whole-Meat Roast Cooking Alternatives

Adapted from the FDA Food Code 2022 edition, §3-401.11(B) and
§3-501.16(A)(1). Teaching data, not current guidance.

## Alternative time and temperature combinations
Whole meat roasts — including beef, corned beef, lamb, pork, and cured pork
roasts such as ham — may be cooked using alternative combinations of a
lower temperature held for a longer time. All parts of the food must reach
the chosen temperature and hold it for the corresponding time: 130°F
(54.4°C) held for 112 minutes, 131°F (55°C) for 89 minutes, 133°F (56.1°C)
for 56 minutes, 135°F (57.2°C) for 36 minutes, 140°F (60°C) for 12 minutes,
or 145°F (62.8°C) for 4 minutes. The holding time may include postoven
heat rise.

## Oven requirements
If cooked in an oven, the oven must be preheated to the temperature
specified for the roast's weight and held at that temperature: for a still
dry oven, 350°F (177°C) or more for roasts under 10 pounds and 250°F
(121°C) or more for roasts of 10 pounds or more; for a convection oven,
325°F (163°C) or more under 10 pounds and 250°F (121°C) or more at 10
pounds or more; for a high-humidity oven, 250°F (121°C) or less.

## Hot holding after alternative cooking
A roast cooked by one of these alternative combinations may be hot-held at
130°F (54°C) or above, instead of the 135°F (57°C) minimum that applies to
other hot-held foods.
