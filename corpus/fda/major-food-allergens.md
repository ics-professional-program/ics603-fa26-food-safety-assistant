# FDA Food Code §1-201.10 — Major Food Allergens

Adapted from the FDA Food Code 2022 edition, definition of "major food
allergen" (§1-201.10) and person-in-charge duties (§2-102.11). Teaching
data, not current guidance.

## The nine major food allergens
The 2022 edition defines nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish
(such as bass, flounder, and cod, including crustacean shellfish such as
crab, lobster, and shrimp), tree nuts (such as almonds, pecans, and
walnuts), wheat, peanuts, soybeans, and sesame. Sesame was added as the
ninth major food allergen following the FASTER Act of 2021. A food
ingredient that contains protein derived from one of these foods is also a
major food allergen.

## What is not a major food allergen
Highly refined oils derived from a listed food, and ingredients derived
from such oils, are not major food allergens. Ingredients exempted under
the Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act petition process are
also excluded.

## Employee allergen awareness
The person in charge must ensure that employees are properly trained in
food allergy awareness as it relates to their assigned duties, including
describing foods that contain major allergens and responding to a
customer's allergen questions accurately.
