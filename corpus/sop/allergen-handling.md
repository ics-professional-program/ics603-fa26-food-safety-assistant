# SOP-12 — Allergen Handling (Pacific Market Cafe)

Written in the Institute of Child Nutrition HACCP-based SOP format and
following its procedure "Preventing Cross-Contamination During Storage and
Preparation" (2018). The ICN set has no allergen SOP, so the allergen list
below comes from the FDA Food Code 2022 edition. Pacific Market Cafe is a
fictional establishment. Teaching data, not current guidance.

Purpose: to prevent an allergic reaction by keeping the major food allergens
out of any order prepared for a guest who has declared an allergy. Scope:
this procedure applies to every employee who takes, prepares, or runs an
order.

## The Big 9 allergens
The nine major food allergens are milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish,
tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame. All menu items containing
them are flagged in the recipe binder.

## Preventing cross-contact
Prepare allergen-free orders first, on a cleaned and sanitized surface,
using the purple-handled allergen utensil kit. Wash hands and change gloves
before starting an allergen-free order. Never use shared fryer oil for an
allergen-free order, and never touch a ready-to-eat allergen-free item with
bare hands.

Store allergen-free ingredients away from and above the foods they must not
touch, in covered and labeled containers, following SOP-01 — Receiving and
Storage.

## Guest allergen questions
Never guess about ingredients. Check the recipe binder, and have the
manager on duty confirm the answer with the guest before the order is fired.
Where a substitution cannot be made safely, say so rather than attempt it.

## Corrective action
Discard any allergen-free order that contacts an allergen at any point and
prepare it again on clean equipment. Report a suspected reaction to the
manager on duty immediately, and call emergency services if the guest shows
any sign of a severe reaction. Retrain any employee found not following this
procedure.

## Verification and record keeping
The manager on duty confirms each allergen order at the pass and records the
item, the declared allergen, and the employee who prepared it on the
allergen order log. Discarded orders are recorded on the damaged or
discarded product log. The general manager reviews and initials the allergen
order log daily, and both logs are kept on file for a minimum of one year.
