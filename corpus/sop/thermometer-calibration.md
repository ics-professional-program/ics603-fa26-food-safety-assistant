# SOP-07 — Thermometer Calibration (Pacific Market Cafe)

Adapted from the Institute of Child Nutrition HACCP-based standard operating
procedure "Using and Calibrating Thermometers" (2018), written for Pacific
Market Cafe, a fictional establishment. Teaching data, not current guidance.

Purpose: to make sure every internal temperature recorded in this kitchen is
measured with a thermometer known to be accurate. Scope: this procedure
applies to every employee who cooks, cools, holds, or receives food.

## Thermometer selection and care
Use a food thermometer that reads from 0°F (-18°C) to 220°F (104°C) and is
appropriate for what is being measured. Measure thin products — burger
patties, chicken breasts, fish fillets — with a thermistor or thermocouple
with a thin probe. A bimetallic dial-stem thermometer is accurate only in
food at least as thick as the dimple mark on its stem. Use only oven-safe
thermometers inside an oven.

Keep thermometers accessible on the line during all hours of operation,
clean and sanitize each probe before every use, and store probes where they
cannot be contaminated.

## Calibration frequency
Calibrate every probe thermometer daily before opening, after it is dropped,
after it is used to measure an extreme temperature (for example, inside an
oven or a freezer), and whenever a reading is in question.

## Ice-point method
Fill a container with crushed ice and cold water. Insert the probe at least
2 inches without touching the container, let it sit for about 1 minute, and
wait until the reading stabilizes. The reading should be 32°F (0°C). Adjust
the thermometer to read 32°F if it does not.

## Boiling-point method
Where the ice-point method is impractical, immerse at least the first two
inches of the probe in boiling water and wait for the reading to stabilize.
The reading should be 212°F (100°C), and reads lower at altitude. Adjust the
thermometer following the manufacturer's instructions.

## Corrective action
Adjust an inaccurate dial-faced thermometer by turning the dial while
holding the calibration nut beneath the dial with pliers. Adjust an
inaccurate digital thermometer with its reset button, following the
manufacturer's instructions. Retrain any employee found calibrating a
thermometer improperly.

## Records and tolerance
Record each calibration on the calibration log. A thermometer that cannot be
adjusted to within ±2°F of 32°F must be removed from service and replaced.
The manager on duty reviews and initials the calibration log daily, and the
log is kept on file for a minimum of one year.
