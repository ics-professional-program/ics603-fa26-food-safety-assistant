# FDA Food Code §2-201 — Employee Health, Exclusions, and Restrictions

Adapted from the FDA Food Code 2022 edition, §§2-201.11, 2-201.12, and
2-201.13. Teaching data, not current guidance.

## Reporting symptoms and diagnoses
A food employee must report to the person in charge any of the symptoms and
diagnoses listed below. The person in charge must ensure that employees know
they are required to report, and must act on a report the same day it is
made.

## Symptoms that require exclusion
Exclude a food employee who is symptomatic with vomiting or diarrhea, unless
the symptom comes from a noninfectious condition documented in writing by a
health practitioner. Exclude a food employee who is jaundiced when the onset
of jaundice occurred within the last 7 calendar days, unless a health
practitioner documents in writing that the jaundice is not caused by
hepatitis A virus or another fecal-orally transmitted infection.

## Reportable diagnoses
Six pathogens are reportable: Norovirus, hepatitis A virus, *Shigella* spp.,
Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* (STEC), *Salmonella* Typhi, and nontyphoidal
*Salmonella*. An employee diagnosed with any of them is excluded, and the
person in charge notifies the regulatory authority.

## Returning to work
A food employee excluded for vomiting or diarrhea may be reinstated when
they have been asymptomatic for at least 24 hours, or when a health
practitioner documents in writing that the symptoms came from a
noninfectious condition. Reinstatement after a reportable diagnosis, after
jaundice, or after typhoid fever additionally requires the approval of the
regulatory authority, and in several cases negative stool specimen cultures.
