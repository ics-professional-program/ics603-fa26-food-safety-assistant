# SOP-01 — Receiving and Storage (Pacific Market Cafe)

Adapted from the Institute of Child Nutrition HACCP-based standard operating
procedures "Receiving Deliveries" and "Preventing Cross-Contamination During
Storage and Preparation" (2018), written for Pacific Market Cafe, a
fictional establishment. Teaching data, not current guidance.

Purpose: to ensure that all food arrives safe and moves into proper storage
as quickly as possible. Scope: this procedure applies to every employee who
receives deliveries or stocks the walk-in and dry storage.

## Before the delivery arrives
Schedule deliveries during operating hours and post the schedule with vendor
names, days, times, and drivers' names. Clear space in the walk-in, freezer,
and dry storage before the truck arrives. Gather the purchase order, the
receiving log, a calibrated probe thermometer, and a clean cart. Confirm the
vendor and the driver against the posted schedule before accepting anything;
if the driver's name differs from the schedule, call the vendor.

## Receiving temperatures
Check every refrigerated delivery with a calibrated probe thermometer; see
SOP-07 — Thermometer Calibration. For fresh meat, fish, and poultry, insert
the probe into the center of the product. For packaged products, place the
probe between two packages without puncturing the wrapper.

Refrigerated TCS foods must arrive at 41°F (5°C) or below. Milk may arrive
at 45°F (7°C) or below but must be stored at 41°F. Frozen foods must arrive
frozen solid, with no large ice crystals, no liquid in the bottom of the
carton, and no other sign of thawing and refreezing.

Inspect the truck for cleanliness and odor, check the integrity of the
packaging, and check the use-by dates on milk, eggs, and other perishables.

## Cold storage order
Store food in the walk-in from top shelf to bottom shelf in this order:
ready-to-eat foods, whole cuts of beef and pork, ground meats, and raw
poultry on the bottom shelf. The order follows cooking temperature — the
food that needs the highest cooking temperature sits lowest, so nothing can
drip onto a food that will be cooked to a lower temperature or eaten as is.
Never store raw animal foods above ready-to-eat foods.

## FIFO rotation
Use first-in, first-out (FIFO) rotation. Label every container with the
product name and the date received or prepared; place newer stock behind
older stock. Store damaged goods in a separate, marked location until they
are returned or discarded.

## Corrective action
Reject and return any delivery that is above temperature, shows signs of
previous thawing, arrives in punctured packaging or dirty crates, is past
its use-by date, or arrives in cans with swollen ends, flawed seams or
seals, deep dents, or rust. Record every rejection on the receiving log, and
retrain any employee found accepting a delivery that should have been
rejected.

## Verification and record keeping
Employees record delivery temperatures and any corrective action on the
receiving log. The manager on duty verifies receiving by observing the
practice during the shift and by reviewing the receiving log at the close of
each day. Receiving logs are kept on file for a minimum of one year.
