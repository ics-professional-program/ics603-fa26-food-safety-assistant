# SOP-09 — Hot Holding and Line Checks (Pacific Market Cafe)

Adapted from the Institute of Child Nutrition HACCP-based standard operating
procedure "Hot and Cold Holding for Time/Temperature Control for Safety
Foods" (2018). Pacific Market Cafe is a fictional establishment; the house
standard below is stricter than the Food Code and is the cafe's own policy.
Teaching data, not current guidance.

Purpose: to prevent foodborne illness by holding every time/temperature
control for safety (TCS) food at the proper temperature. Scope: this
procedure applies to every employee who prepares, holds, or serves food on
the line. Where this SOP is stricter than the FDA Food Code or the Hawaii
Food Safety Code, the SOP governs company operations.

## Company hot-holding standard
Hold all hot TCS foods at 140°F (60°C) or above on the line. This house
standard is deliberately stricter than the regulatory minimum of 135°F
(57°C): the extra five degrees gives the line margin, so a brief dip during
a rush is caught by a line check before the food ever falls below the
regulatory limit.

Preheat steam tables and hot boxes, and verify that the air or water
temperature of the unit is at 140°F or above before any food goes into it.
Food that has not yet reached 140°F is reheated first — a holding unit is
not a cooking device.

## Line checks
Use a clean, sanitized, and calibrated probe thermometer; see SOP-07 —
Thermometer Calibration. Take the internal temperature near the surface of
the product, at the thickest part, and at one other location in the pan.

Probe every hot-held pan at opening and then every 2 hours thereafter, and
record each reading on the line-check log.

## Corrective action
Reheat a pan reading below 140°F but at or above 135°F rapidly to 165°F
(74°C) for 15 seconds and return it to holding, provided the last recorded
reading was at or above 140°F and was taken within the last 2 hours. Repair
or reset the holding unit before the pan goes back in.

Discard any pan reading below 135°F, and any pan whose time below
temperature cannot be determined from the log. Record the discard on the
damaged or discarded product log. Retrain any employee found not following
this procedure.

## Verification and record keeping
Employees record every line-check temperature and any corrective action on
the line-check log. The manager on duty verifies the checks by observing the
line during the shift and by reviewing, initialing, and dating the log at
the close of each day. Line-check logs are kept on file for a minimum of one
year.
